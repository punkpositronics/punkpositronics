I had a wonderful brand new home NAS setup for about 4 months. Then the motherboard started making this awful whine/vibrating constantly. At first, I told myself the fans were dirty, and it would be ok when I had a chance to properly clean out the dust. When I did, and it immediately made the same notice upon plugging it in and hitting power… I was disheartened. It had taken all of my old IT skills nearly 7 years removed from working in the field, to open this device, take out the fans, clean them and reassemble it.

If it wasn’t the fans, then what could it be? While dealing with the constant annoyance I sought ideas on Reddit and the like. So many possibilities. Next I tried to check if the hard drives were vibrating against the case. One by one I tried to hold them tighter, then removing them safely. Finally, I was fed up and decided I would wipe it all and start over if need be, and I removed all the HDDs at once. 5x 3.5mm Iron Wolfs. Surely the weight wasn’t secure and this would stop the noise? Hit power and immediately still the cacophony of noise rush into my ears. How?

Determined to get to the bottom of this I decided since the HDDs were out I may as well start looking for the exact spot this noise is originating from. Piece by piece I stripped it down. Nothing helped. It was not the case, or the brackets. It was not loose cable or connections. Finally, I held only the motherboard with power supplied. Still the noise would not subside.

It was at that point I put it all loose in a bankers box and decided it needed a new motherboard. Then I found out Synology motherboards are proprietary and they DO NOT sell replacements. So now I had a box of IT junk and all my data on useless HDDs. It was then my wife and I decided it was a dead motherboard already may as well try anything, its already broke rights? I did all the research I could stomach and decided it was coming from the CPU - maybe it was loose? What did I know I had never had to do this before?

I yanked off the heat sinks and then powered it again to check. Still the noise. Did you know you can't solder heat sinks back onto the CPU? Yeah, at the time neither did I. Suffice to say I failed that day but learned many new lessons. But the main thing that came from it is this: We should have the right to be able to repair own devices & a middle man sucking money from us should not hold the keys to the HDDs housing all MY data. But what to do about it? I put it all in a box and walked away for the time being. There would be no server replacement at those prices either. I was between a rock and a hard place. So the choice was to go without for the immediate future.

Sound familiar? You still safely own your digital data, photos, movies, music, books etc. but you can’t use it, even access it, without subscription A or expensive service B? I was sick of it. There had to be a better way...
